1. Spread of chick weights (chickwts)

The chickwts data records the weight (grams) of 71 chicks after six weeks on one of six feeds. The standard deviation (sd) measures spread around the mean; the interquartile range (IQR) is the width of the middle 50% of the data, Q(0.75) − Q(0.25).

Takeaways. Horsebean chicks vary least; casein and meatmeal chicks vary most. Sunflower has a middling SD (48.8) but the smallest IQR (27.5): one unusually light chick (153 g) inflates the SD, while the IQR ignores it. This is the robustness idea from section 13.2.6. The overall values (mean 261.31, SD 78.07, IQR 119) match the chapter.

Covariance needs two numeric variables, and chickwts has only one (weight; feed is categorical), so covariance is shown with quakes below.

2. Covariance and correlation (quakes)

The quakes data holds 1,000 seismic events off Fiji. Plotting the number of stations reporting each event against its magnitude shows how the two variables move together.

Takeaways. The covariance is positive, so stations and magnitude rise together, and a correlation of 0.851 indicates a strong positive linear association. The vertical stripes appear because magnitudes were recorded to one decimal place. Correlation measures association only; it does not show that magnitude causes the station count.
